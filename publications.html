Research in print.

Work in Progress

Under review

  • Ng, C.-T., Chen, X.-Y., Cho, Y.-C., Qin, S., Chen, S.-F., Chao, C.-C., Liou, M., & Chang, T.-T. (under review). Cortisol responses distinguish affective and neurocognitive profiles during timed arithmetic.

    We examined how individual differences in cortisol responses relate to decision-making, subjective affect, and neural responses during demanding arithmetic tasks.

In preparation

  • Ng, C.-T., Chen, X.-Y., Huang, P.-H., & Chang, T.-T. (in preparation). Confident but anxious? Rethinking the link between self-efficacy and test anxiety across genders.

    In a secondary analysis of data from 9,078 university students in the SMARVUS dataset, we examined whether the association between general self-efficacy and test anxiety differs by gender after accounting for trait anxiety.

  • Chui, K., & Ng, C.-T. (in preparation). Integrating meanings across modalities: Early gesture-speech representation of entities and events.

    We examined how Mandarin-speaking children aged 8–24 months integrate gesture and speech to express entity and event meanings as their spoken language develops.

  • Ng, G. T. T., Au, R. H. Y., & Ng, C.-T. (in preparation). When the Bible Soothes: Experimental effects of supportive scripture on personal stressors.

    Across three randomised experiments, we investigated how reading supportive biblical passages influences distress, perceived solvability, and motivation when reflecting on unresolved personal difficulties.

  • Chang, T.-T., Liu, S.-H., Jan, N.-Y., Ng, C.-T., Chen, X.-Y., Cho, Y.-C., Chiang, C.-Y., Lin, Y.-R., Hsu, M.-C., & Li, Y.-H. (in preparation). Beyond the next token: Advancing human-AI hybrid intelligence through modular cognitive architectures.

    We propose a neurocognitively informed framework for human–AI collaboration that draws on specialised brain systems and cognitive control to support mathematical reasoning and learning.
