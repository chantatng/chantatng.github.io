Development in context.

Mathematical cognition and learning

I study how numerical representations and arithmetic skills develop, and how the brain systems that support them change with age. Using behavioural experiments, fMRI, and computational modelling, I investigate how people process quantities in symbolic and nonsymbolic formats and how these processes relate to mathematical performance.

Selected work

  • Distinct behavioural and brain response profiles during arithmetic and sentence comprehension
  • Frontoparietal and salience network synchronizations during nonsymbolic magnitude processing

Emotion and adaptive cognition

I study how anxiety, stress, and emotion regulation relate to learning, performance, and responses to everyday difficulties. My work includes studies of individual differences in math anxiety, evaluations of digital mathematics training, and research on how children’s emotion regulation relates to their experiences at school and to their well-being. In related work, my collaborators and I examine how reading supportive scripture influences people’s responses to unresolved personal stressors.

Selected work

  • Evaluation of math anxiety and its remediation through digital mathematics training
  • Cortisol responses during timed arithmetic (under review)
  • Supportive scripture and responses to personal stressors (in preparation)

Language, gesture, and multimodal communication

In collaboration with Professor Kawai Chui, I study how young Mandarin-speaking children combine gesture and speech as their spoken language develops. I also examine the brain systems that support co-speech gesture processing in adults.

Selected work

  • Do gestures compensate for language deficiency in interactional discourse?
  • The visuo-sensorimotor substrate of co-speech gesture processing
