Development in context.

Much of my research focuses on children, from early gesture and speech to how school-age learners work with numbers and respond to challenges.

Mathematical cognition and learning

I study how numerical representations and arithmetic skills develop, including the brain systems that support them. In one line of work, children and adults compare quantities shown as digits or dot arrays. I use behavioural experiments, fMRI and computational modelling to examine how number understanding and task performance change with age.

Selected work

  • Distinct behavioural and brain response profiles during arithmetic and sentence comprehension
  • Frontoparietal and salience network synchronizations during nonsymbolic magnitude processing

Emotion and adaptive cognition

I study how people respond to stress in learning and everyday life. In work with children, I have examined individual differences in math anxiety and evaluated digital maths training. I also study how strategies such as reappraisal or expressive suppression relate to children’s engagement and well-being. With collaborators, I examine how reading supportive scripture affects people’s responses to personal stressors.

Selected work

  • Evaluation of math anxiety and its remediation through digital mathematics training
  • Cortisol responses during timed arithmetic (under review)
  • Supportive scripture and responses to personal stressors (in preparation)

Language, gesture, and multimodal communication

In collaboration with Professor Kawai Chui, I contribute quantitative analyses to studies of how young Mandarin-speaking children combine gesture and speech during naturalistic play with their mothers. Our recent work includes children aged 8–24 months. We also study how adults process gestures that accompany speech and are designing experiments on gesture and concepts of time.

Selected work

  • Do gestures compensate for language deficiency in interactional discourse?
  • The visuo-sensorimotor substrate of co-speech gesture processing
